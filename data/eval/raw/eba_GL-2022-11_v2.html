EBA Guidelines on CDD risk factors (EBA/GL/2022/11) — AMENDED by EBA/GL/2024/11

15 August 2024
EBA/GL/2024/11 amending EBA/GL/2022/11

These amended guidelines update the CDD risk factor guidelines originally issued in November 2022. The amendments reflect developments in the ML/TF risk landscape, including the emergence of crypto-asset risks and updated FATF Recommendations. They apply from 1 January 2025.

Section 1: Risk factor categories [UNCHANGED]

Institutions shall assess risk across four dimensions: (a) customer risk factors; (b) product, service, transaction or delivery channel risk factors; (c) geographic risk factors; (d) other risk factors including the quality of the customer's home-country AML/CFT regime.

Section 2: Simplified due diligence conditions [AMENDED]

Simplified due diligence may be applied only where an institution has documented evidence that the business relationship or transaction presents a lower degree of risk. [AMENDED: removed virtual asset service providers from the list of potentially lower-risk customer types; VASPs now require standard or enhanced due diligence at all times]

Section 2a: Crypto-asset specific risk factors [NEW SECTION]

When assessing the ML/TF risk associated with virtual asset transactions, institutions shall consider: (a) whether the counterparty is a registered VASP; (b) the proportion of transactions involving privacy-enhancing coins or mixing services; (c) the transaction's exposure to high-risk jurisdictions as defined in the FATF list; (d) the customer's explanation for use of virtual assets versus traditional payment rails.

Section 3: Enhanced due diligence triggers [AMENDED]

Enhanced due diligence is mandatory in the following circumstances [AMENDED — two new triggers added]: (a) business relationships with high-risk third countries; (b) customers identified as politically exposed persons (PEPs); (c) transactions with no apparent economic or legal purpose; (d) complex or unusually large transactions; (e) customers active in virtual asset markets with transaction volumes exceeding EUR 10,000 per month [NEW]; (f) customers using privacy-enhancing technologies for payment or settlement [NEW].

Section 4: Correspondent banking [UNCHANGED]

For correspondent banking relationships, institutions shall obtain sufficient information about the respondent institution to understand fully the nature of its business, assess its AML/CFT controls, and determine that it is not a shell bank.

These amended guidelines shall apply from 1 January 2025.
