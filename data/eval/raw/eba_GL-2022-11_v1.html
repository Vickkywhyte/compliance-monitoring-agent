EBA Guidelines on CDD risk factors (EBA/GL/2022/11) — ORIGINAL

1 November 2022
EBA/GL/2022/11

These guidelines set out the factors that obliged entities and competent authorities should take into account when assessing the money laundering and terrorist financing (ML/TF) risk associated with individual business relationships and occasional transactions.

They apply to credit institutions, payment institutions, electronic money institutions, and investment firms. Compliance is required from 1 May 2023.

Section 1: Risk factor categories

Institutions shall assess risk across four dimensions: (a) customer risk factors; (b) product, service, transaction or delivery channel risk factors; (c) geographic risk factors; (d) other risk factors including the quality of the customer's home-country AML/CFT regime.

Section 2: Simplified due diligence conditions

Simplified due diligence may be applied only where an institution has documented evidence that the business relationship or transaction presents a lower degree of risk. The following are non-exhaustive examples of potentially lower-risk scenarios: (a) regulated financial institutions as customers; (b) listed companies subject to disclosure requirements; (c) public administrations and government bodies.

Section 3: Enhanced due diligence triggers

Enhanced due diligence is mandatory in the following circumstances: (a) business relationships with high-risk third countries; (b) customers identified as politically exposed persons (PEPs); (c) transactions with no apparent economic or legal purpose; (d) complex or unusually large transactions.

Section 4: Correspondent banking

For correspondent banking relationships, institutions shall obtain sufficient information about the respondent institution to understand fully the nature of its business, assess its AML/CFT controls, and determine that it is not a shell bank.

These guidelines shall apply from 1 May 2023.
